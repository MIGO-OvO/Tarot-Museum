# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Serious tarot students — readers who study symbolism, numerology (毕达哥拉斯灵数), astrology, alchemy, and the Kabbalistic Tree of Life in depth. They arrive in a focused, study-minded state and stay to read long-form scholarly content in Chinese (zh-CN), with English and Hebrew terms woven throughout. The job to be done: understand each card's hidden meaning across multiple interpretive dimensions, treating the site as an authoritative reference manuscript rather than a quick-lookup tool.

## Product Purpose
To present a static-HTML scholarly exhibition of all 78 tarot cards (22 Major + 56 Minor Arcana), structured as a curated "manuscript hall" (The Arcanum Gallery) with a gallery index and per-card deep-dive pages under the `cards/` directory.

## Brand Personality
Scholarly, reverent, archival. The tone is that of a museum curator or manuscript annotator — confident, quiet, and bilingual (Chinese with Latin/Hebrew terms). The goal is a museum-hushed space inviting contemplation.

## Anti-references
- **Not New-Age kitsch** — no purple-galaxy gradients, neon mysticism, glitter, or clip-art crystals and moons.
- **Not generic SaaS / AI** — no flat cards, Inter-everywhere, rounded-everything, default startup landing-page look.
- **Not loud or commercial** — no hard-sell CTAs, popups, exclamation energy, or marketplace vibes.
- **Not sterile / clinical** — never strip away the warmth, ornament, and paper texture in the name of minimalism.

## Design Principles
1. **Manuscript, not app.** Every element should read as part of a printed exhibition catalogue (page rhythm, plate numbers, hairline rules) rather than web UI chrome.
2. **Quiet authority.** Earn trust through restraint, accuracy, and craft. No persuasion tactics; the content's depth is the draw. Whitespace and stillness are features.
3. **Two precious accents, used sparingly.** Gold (`--gold` / `--gold-deep`) for ornament and reverence, wine (`--wine` / `--wine-soft`) for emphasis and interaction. Their power comes from scarcity against warm paper.
4. **Bilingual hierarchy with intent.** Serif Chinese carries the prose; mono Latin/Hebrew carries labels and esoterica. Respect both scripts' legibility and letter-spacing conventions.
5. **Motion that turns the page.** Animation is slow, eased, and purposeful, revealing content as the reader scrolls. It should feel like turning a page, never like an app transition.
6. **Reverence in the details.** Inset frames, ornament glyphs, considered spacing, and accessible contrast (WCAG-conscious) are key to maintaining quality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Maintain WCAG-conscious contrast (body text ≥4.5:1 against aged-paper backgrounds) even within the warm low-chroma palette. Support reduced motion preferences.
